%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
\revtext{Transfer of landslide susceptibility models to newly studied basins is routinely assessed with conventions that overstate it. We quantify five of them on one benchmark---14 Chilean watersheds, ten source basins spanning the Atacama-to-Patagonia gradient and four semi-arid targets in north-central Chile (27--31$^\circ$S)---using few-shot meta-learning as the paradigm under scrutiny and a 30\,m MERIT-DEM feature stack. Random cross-validation inflates $F_1$ by up to 21\,pp in heterogeneous basins; fixed-threshold evaluation hides that precision at 5\% prevalence is 0.05--0.09; pooling correlated episodes narrows confidence intervals $3.3\times$; selecting features with target-basin labels doubles the apparent few-shot advantage (from $+2.4$ to $+4.7$\,pp at $K=1$, identical seeds); and comparing episodic meta-training against a size-weighted pooled baseline credits the algorithm with an effect of domain weighting. Under the corrected protocol---source-only feature selection, spatially disjoint and buffered validation, partition-level inference, five seeds---a meta-learned initialisation exceeds training from scratch at one labelled event per class by $+4.3$\,pp $F_1$ jointly across the targets ($p<0.001$; significant in three of four basins after Holm correction), by $+1.6$\,pp at five events and by nothing at ten; an independent replication gives $+2.2$\,pp. Pooled pre-training reproduces the advantage once source basins are weighted equally ($-0.04$\,pp between episodic and balanced pooled training), a pooled-source logistic regression matches or exceeds every neural method at $K=1$, and adversarial domain adaptation offers no few-shot gain. Benchmark, data and protocol are released.}
\end{abstract}
