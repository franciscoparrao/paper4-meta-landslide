%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Landslide susceptibility mapping in \emph{newly-studied} basins is limited by the cost of local inventories, and the transfer of models from data-rich basins is routinely assessed with protocols that overstate it. We quantify that overstatement---random cross-validation inflates $F_1$ by 5--27\,pp over spatially disjoint validation, balanced test sets hide that precision at operational prevalence falls to 0.07--0.10, and pooling correlated episodes narrows confidence intervals by $3.4\times$---and show that under rigorous evaluation a \emph{transferable geomorphic prior} nonetheless exists, which episodic meta-training extracts where naive pooled pretraining fails. We benchmark first-order meta-learning (Reptile, FOMAML) against Independent, fine-tuning, domain-adversarial and classical baselines across 14 Chilean basins spanning $\sim$32$^\circ$ of latitude ($\sim$1 to $\sim$1{,}900\,mm/yr), evaluating $K$-shot transfer ($K \in \{1, 5, 10, 20\}$) on four targets from a 30\,m MERIT-DEM stack. Under spatially disjoint validation, meta-learning outperforms Independent at $K=1$ in all four targets (mean lift $+5.5$ to $+6.2$ percentage points $F_1$), significant under a cluster-level paired test in three of four; the smallest ($N=19$) has too few spatial partitions to resolve it. The advantage decays monotonically and vanishes by $K=20$, and absolute discrimination remains modest in two heterogeneous targets ($F_1 \approx 0.5$). A pooled-source logistic regression is competitive at every $K$: the contribution is the prior itself and the narrow few-shot regime where it pays off, not a new state-of-the-art predictor. Domain-adversarial adaptation improves on Independent in no target. The benchmark, datasets and protocol are released to support low-resource Earth-observation transfer in mountain-hazard monitoring.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Random cross-validation inflates cross-basin transfer F1 by 5-27 percentage points.

\item Meta-learning beats Independent at K=1 in 4/4 targets, significant in 3/4.
\item Episodic meta-training extracts a transferable geomorphic prior; naive pooling fails.
\item Pooled logistic regression stays competitive: the finding is the prior itself.
\end{highlights}

Lines: mean across $40$ episodes $\times$ 3 seeds; shaded bands: bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals. Meta-learning (Reptile or FOMAML) outperforms Independent at $K=1$ in all four basins; Reptile leads at $K=1$ in Elqui and Limar\'i, FOMAML leads in Huasco, and the two are within overlapping CIs in Copiap\'o. FOMAML achieves $+5.5$\,pp at $K=1$, decaying monotonically to $-1.1$\,pp at $K=20$ (over the three basins where $K=20$ is feasible); Reptile decays from $+6.2$ to $+1.9$\,pp. DANN's advantage is negative at $K \leq 10$ ($-0.5$ to $-1.4$\,pp) and $+1.4$\,pp at $K=20$.
