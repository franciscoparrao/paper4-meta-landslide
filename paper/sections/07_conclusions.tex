\revtext{We asked how much the standard conventions for evaluating cross-basin landslide susceptibility transfer overstate it, and what survives when they are removed, using few-shot meta-learning across 14 Chilean watersheds---ten source basins spanning the Atacama-to-Patagonia gradient and four semi-arid target basins of north-central Chile---as the test case. Five conventions were isolated on identical data. Random cross-validation inflates $F_1$ by up to 21~pp in the heterogeneous basins and not at all in one basin. Balanced evaluation at a fixed threshold hides that precision at a 5\% prevalence is $0.05$--$0.09$ for every method. Pooling correlated episodes narrows confidence intervals $3.3\times$. Selecting features with the labels of the target basins doubled the few-shot advantage of meta-learning, from $+2.4$ to $+4.7$~pp at $K=1$ under identical seeds, and was the reason the submitted version of this work reported a larger effect. And comparing episodic meta-training against a size-weighted pooled baseline, as is customary, attributes to the meta-learning algorithm an advantage that belongs to domain weighting.}

\revtext{Under the strict protocol---source-only feature selection, spatially disjoint and buffered validation, partition-level inference with a pre-specified joint hypothesis, five seeds---three findings remain:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \revtext{\textbf{A source-learned initialisation helps in the extreme few-shot regime, and the effect is modest.} Reptile exceeds training from scratch at $K=1$ by $+4.3$~pp $F_1$ jointly across the four targets ($p<0.001$; $+2.6$ to $+7.4$~pp per basin, significant in three of four after Holm correction, not in Huasco where the spatial partitions coincide), by $+1.6$~pp at $K=5$, and by nothing at $K \geq 10$. The gain in ROC-AUC ($+0.05$ to $+0.14$) and in prevalence-weighted average precision survives re-weighting to realistic prevalence, and the advantage is insensitive to exclusion buffers of up to 10~km and attenuates to $+3.6$~pp at 20~km. An independent replication of the protocol gives $+2.2$~pp, and one-hot encoding the one categorical input halves the three-seed estimate, so the honest statement is an advantage of about 2--4~pp at one labelled event per class, whose magnitude depends on run-to-run and preprocessing choices by a factor of two.}
    \item \revtext{\textbf{Domain weighting, not episodic optimisation, is what transfers.} Pooled pre-training with every source basin weighted equally reproduces the meta-learning advantage; given balanced weighting, episodic training and pooled training are indistinguishable ($-0.04$~pp over four targets and five seeds), and a frozen size-weighted encoder fails. The customary Fine-tune baseline underperformed because one inventory supplied half of its training data, not because pooled pre-training cannot transfer. A pooled-source logistic regression on nine DEM descriptors matches or exceeds every neural method at $K=1$ in all four basins.}
    \item \revtext{\textbf{Domain-adversarial alignment offers no few-shot advantage} with ten source domains: DANN and CDAN equal Independent at $K=1$ and DANN is weakly positive only at $K=5$.}
\end{enumerate}

\revtext{Limitations include the use of a small tabular MLP backbone (a spatial CNN improves $F_1$ by 6--11~pp on one basin), the restriction of the evaluated transfer to four semi-arid targets, mixed triggers and movement types in the inventories, one polygon inventory supplying half of the source positives, and a between-run variance of the meta-training procedure that is of the same order as the effect. Future work should extend the evaluation to humid and Patagonian targets and to U-Net architectures across several basins, and should treat trigger-aware meta-learning as the natural response to intra-basin heterogeneity of the Elqui kind.}

\revtext{For practitioners, the recommendation is deliberately modest and now rests on a comparison that controls for the confounds above: start a new basin from a pooled-source logistic regression or a basin-balanced pooled network on open DEM descriptors, validate with spatially disjoint and buffered folds, report uncertainty at the partition level, and expect a first-pass susceptibility layer that ranks terrain usefully but whose per-pixel precision at operational prevalence is low. The methodological lesson generalises beyond landslides: a cross-site transfer benchmark should be built so that the test sites contribute nothing---no labels, no features, no weighting---to the model before the moment of evaluation, and should state how much each relaxation of that rule would have added to the reported skill. The released benchmark, datasets and protocol are intended to make that standard reproducible in other data-scarce regions of Latin America and beyond.}
