\subsection{Basins}
\label{sec:data:basins}

We study 14 watersheds distributed along the length of Chile (Figure~\ref{fig:study_area}), from the hyper-arid Atacama Desert ($\sim$21$^\circ$S) to Patagonia ($\sim$53$^\circ$S), selected to span an extreme hydro-climatic gradient (basin-mean annual precipitation $\sim$1 to $\sim$1{,}900\,mm/yr, with individual pixels in the wettest basin exceeding 2{,}500\,mm/yr) and to have heterogeneous landslide-mapping coverage. \textbf{Ten source basins} are used for meta-training: Cha\~naral / R\'io Salado (BNA~032, hyper-arid; \revtext{800 landslide and 910 non-landslide points from the supplementary data of \citet{parra2025tesis}, mapped after the March~2015 Atacama floods}), Costeras Q.~Negra--Pan de Az\'ucar / Taltal (BNA~029, hyper-arid coastal; 80 mass-movement polygons + 300 negatives from \citet{parra2025tesis}), Costeras Tilviche-Loa (BNA~018; 127~events), Costeras Loa-Caracoles (BNA~022; 50~events), R\'io Maipo (BNA~057; 349~events), R\'io Rapel (BNA~060; 392~events), R\'io Maule (BNA~073, semi-humid; the 203 of the 1{,}227 events in the 2010 $M_w$ 8.8 earthquake-induced inventory of \citet{serey2019maule} that fall within the basin polygon, retaining all four mapped typologies---disrupted slides, flows, lateral spreads and coherent slides), R\'io Choapa (BNA~047, semi-arid; 62~events from the SERNAGEOMIN national \emph{catastro de remociones en masa}), Costeras Bueno-Puelo (BNA~104; 47~events), and Costeras Magallanes (BNA~125; 413~events). \textbf{Four target basins} are used for $K$-shot evaluation: R\'io Copiap\'o (BNA~034; \revtext{21 catastro events, 19 after snapping to the grid}), R\'io Huasco (BNA~038; \revtext{296 events, 278 pixels}), R\'io Elqui (BNA~043; \revtext{141 events, 132 pixels}), and R\'io Limar\'i (BNA~045; \revtext{42 events, 35 pixels}).

\revtext{The four targets are the semi-arid basins of north-central Chile (27--31$^\circ$S) that the funding programme designates as priority basins for susceptibility mapping, and the only basins in the national catastro with inventories independent of the source data. The source pool, not the target set, spans the Atacama-to-Patagonia gradient: the source basins supply the climatic and lithological diversity from which a transferable initialisation is learned, whereas transfer is \emph{evaluated} only on semi-arid targets. Transfer to humid or Patagonian basins is therefore not tested here, and the conclusions are stated for the evaluated targets. Table~\ref{tab:basin_summary} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) summarises, for every basin, the inventory source and geometry, the number of mapped events, the negative-sampling rule and the dominant trigger reported by the inventory. Two properties of this dataset bound the generality of the results and are returned to in Section~\ref{sec:disc:limits}: the inventories mix rainfall- and earthquake-triggered movements of several types, and one polygon inventory (Taltal) contributes 49\% of the source positives.}

The satellite-derived MERIT DEM and its terrain, hydrology and texture derivatives supply the bulk of the stack. MERIT DEM is preferred over individual SRTM tiles because its hydrologic corrections (pit filling, stream burning) yield more stable flow-routing derivatives essential for HAND and sediment connectivity. WorldClim 2.1 captures the 1970--2000 climatological baseline; while static climate features cannot represent extreme events that trigger individual landslides, they parameterize the long-term hydro-climatic regime that conditions susceptibility \citep{ma2025metalsm}. All raster inputs are resampled bilinearly (continuous variables) or by nearest neighbor (categorical), then stacked into a per-pixel feature vector consumed by the classifier. We acknowledge that this introduces an effective-resolution mismatch: MERIT-derived terrain and texture features inherit the $\sim$90~m source resolution despite being sampled on the 30~m grid, and WorldClim bioclimatics are nearly piecewise-constant within each $\sim$5~km native cell. Topographic and texture derivatives can thus be expected to be smoother than a native 30~m DEM (e.g., Copernicus GLO-30) would yield, and the climate-distance analysis of Section~\ref{sec:results:climate} is interpreted at basin level rather than per pixel. We return to this limitation in Section~\ref{sec:disc:limits}. Intermediate per-basin products can be regenerated from public sources using the released pipeline (see the Data and code availability statement at the end of the manuscript) to support reproducibility.

\subsection{Conditioning factors}
\label{sec:data:factors}

We compute a common cross-basin stack of \textbf{58 features} per basin at 30~m grid alignment, organized in six categories. To guarantee an identical feature schema across all 14 basins, we retain only features available in every basin (Section~\ref{sec:data:sampling}); Sentinel-2 spectral composites, computed for two coastal source basins (Taltal, Choapa) but not the remaining twelve, are therefore excluded by this intersection and do not enter the benchmark. The 58 retained features are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Terrain (28):} slope, aspect, eastness, northness, hillshade, sky-view factor, openness (positive, negative), geomorphons, landform, Multi-resolution Index of Valley Bottom Flatness (MRVBF), Multi-resolution Index of Ridge-Top Flatness (MRRTF), vector ruggedness measure, deviation from mean elevation, convergence, curvature, curvedness, shape index, surface area ratio, three TPI radii plus TPI, valley depth, relative slope position, LS-factor, TRI.
    \item \textbf{Hydrology (10):} filled DEM, flow accumulation (D8, MFD), flow direction (D8, D$\infty$), HAND, stream network, TWI, drainage density, sediment connectivity \citep{borselli2008sc}.
    \item \textbf{Texture GLCM (6):} contrast, correlation, dissimilarity, energy, entropy, homogeneity, computed on the DEM rather than on optical imagery so that the texture descriptors capture topographic roughness (a physical correlate of slope instability) and remain available in every basin, independent of cloud-free optical coverage.
    \item \textbf{Focal statistics (6):} DEM std at radii 1, 4, 10; DEM range $r=4$; slope std $r=4$; slope mean $r=4$.
    \item \textbf{Climate (5):} WorldClim 2.1 bioclimatics---bio\_01 (annual mean temperature), bio\_12 (annual precipitation), bio\_13/14 (precipitation wettest/driest month), bio\_15 (precipitation seasonality).
    \item \textbf{Geology (3):} SERNAGEOMIN 1:1{,}000{,}000 lithology class, rock type, geological age (categorical, encoded).
\end{itemize}

All factors are computed using SurtGIS, a Rust-based geospatial toolkit \citep{parra2026surtgis}, with reprojection to UTM zone 19S and bilinear/categorical resampling as appropriate.

\subsection{Sampling protocol}
\label{sec:data:sampling}

We adopt a \textbf{hybrid balanced sampling} strategy. Each pixel of the 30~m DEM grid is the unit of analysis. For each basin:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Positives:} For point inventories, snap to nearest pixel \revtext{(coincident points collapse to one pixel, which is why pixel counts are slightly below event counts)}. For polygon inventories (Taltal), rasterize with \texttt{all\_touched=True} and randomly sub-sample at most 30 pixels per polygon to limit spatial autocorrelation.
    \item \textbf{Negatives:} \revtext{Only two basins provide mapped non-landslide points: Cha\~naral (910 points) and Taltal (300 points). There, the mapped points are expanded by a 60~m buffer to cover surrounding pixels and, in Taltal, completed by random sampling to reach the positive count. In the remaining twelve basins, negatives are generated by random sampling within the basin polygon, excluding a 500~m buffer around positives to avoid spatial leakage. The rule applied to each basin is listed in Table~\ref{tab:basin_summary}; the sensitivity of the results to the negative-sampling rule is tested in Sections~\ref{sec:results:spatial} and~\ref{sec:disc:limits}.}
    \item \textbf{Balancing:} Negatives are sub-sampled or augmented to match positive count exactly (1:1).
\end{enumerate}

\revtext{This procedure yields 9{,}182 samples across the 14 basins: 4{,}591 positive and 4{,}591 negative pixels, balanced 1:1 within every basin (per-basin counts in Table~\ref{tab:basin_summary}). The ten source basins contribute 4{,}127 positives, of which Taltal's rasterised polygons supply 2{,}003 (49\%) and Cha\~naral 562 (14\%); the four target basins contribute 464 positives (Copiap\'o 19, Limar\'i 35, Elqui 132, Huasco 278). Target basins are used exclusively for $K$-shot evaluation: no target pixel or label enters meta-training, pre-training, feature selection or the estimation of any preprocessing parameter that uses labels.}

\paragraph{\revtext{Preprocessing}} \revtext{Continuous features are standardised per basin by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation of all sampled pixels of that basin, without using labels. For a target basin these statistics are computed over its sampled pixels, including those that later serve as queries; this is an unsupervised, domain-wise normalisation equivalent to standardising over the basin raster, which is available operationally before any inventory exists, and it uses no label information. Missing values (rare, at raster edges) are imputed with the per-basin feature median. Two of the selected features, landform and geomorphons, are categorical in nature; they were entered as integer class codes, a choice we test by one-hot encoding in Section~\ref{sec:results:preproc}, together with the alternative of estimating the standardisation statistics from the source pool or from the $2K$ support pixels only.}

\subsection{Feature selection via correlation-based feature selection (CFS)}
\label{sec:data:cfs}

We apply \textbf{correlation-based feature selection} \citep{hall1999cfs} using symmetric uncertainty (information-theoretic, robust to mixed data types). Features are quantile-discretized into 10 bins. Forward greedy search adds the feature that maximizes the subset score:
\begin{equation}
    M_S = \frac{k \cdot \overline{r_{cf}}}{\sqrt{k + k(k-1) \cdot \overline{r_{ff}}}},
    \label{eq:cfs}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is subset size, $\overline{r_{cf}}$ the mean feature-class correlation, and $\overline{r_{ff}}$ the mean inter-feature correlation. The procedure terminates when no feature addition improves the score.

\revtext{Feature selection uses the labels of the \textbf{ten source basins only} (8{,}254 samples). Run on the source pool, CFS selects \textbf{9 features}: 5 terrain (landform, openness positive, MRVBF, MRRTF, curvedness), 2 focal statistics (DEM std $r=10$, slope std $r=4$) and 2 texture GLCM (entropy, correlation). No hydrology, geology or climate feature is selected: the discriminative signal shared across the Chilean source inventories is entirely morphometric. In the originally submitted version of this work, CFS had been run on all 14 basins, so that the labels of the four target basins (10\% of the samples) contributed to the choice of features; that run selected two additional features, geomorphons and annual precipitation (bio\_12), both of which carry within-target signal (Table~\ref{tab:cfs_comparison}). Selecting features with target labels is a form of leakage that precedes $K$-shot evaluation, and we quantify its effect on the reported transfer as one of the evaluation conventions studied in Section~\ref{sec:results:leakage}. All results in this paper use the source-only selection.}
