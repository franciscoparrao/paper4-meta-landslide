\subsection{Backbone model}
\label{sec:methods:backbone}

All methods share a 3-layer MLP backbone: \revtext{9} input features $\rightarrow$ 64 hidden $\rightarrow$ 32 hidden $\rightarrow$ 1 output, with ReLU activations, 10\% dropout per hidden layer, and binary cross-entropy with logits as the training loss. We deliberately use a small tabular network rather than a CNN or Transformer because (i) our feature representation is point-wise and scale-invariant, (ii) our pixel-level samples lack spatial neighborhoods at this stage of the pipeline, and (iii) it ensures interpretability of feature importance. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the natural extension to U-Net architectures for spatially-structured prediction.

\subsection{Methods compared}
\label{sec:methods:methods}

\paragraph{Independent} Train MLP from scratch on $K$ target labels ($K$ positives + $K$ negatives), 30 Adam steps at learning rate $10^{-2}$. No source-basin information is used.

\paragraph{Fine-tune (concat-source baseline)} Pretrain MLP on the concatenation of all 10 source basins (300 full-batch Adam steps at $10^{-3}$), then fine-tune for 30 steps on $K$ target samples. \revtext{Because the concatenation is size-weighted, Taltal contributes 49\% of the pre-training positives and the four smallest source basins together 7\%.}

\paragraph{\revtext{Fine-tune (basin-balanced)}} \revtext{Identical to Fine-tune except that the pre-training loss weights every sample by the inverse size of its basin, so that each source basin receives the same total weight as it does under episodic meta-training. This control separates the effect of \emph{domain weighting} from the effect of \emph{episodic optimisation} (Section~\ref{sec:results:weighting}).}

\paragraph{Reptile} \citep{nichol2018reptile}. First-order meta-learning. For 300 outer steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Sample a source basin $\tau$ \revtext{uniformly at random (every basin is equally likely regardless of its size)}.
    \item Sample $K_{\text{meta}}=10$ support points.
    \item Adapt model with 5 inner SGD steps at $10^{-2}$.
    \item Update meta-model: $\theta \leftarrow \theta + \epsilon (\theta_{\text{adapted}} - \theta)$ with $\epsilon = 0.1$.
\end{enumerate}
At test time: load meta-model and adapt for 30 steps on $K$ target samples (same protocol as Fine-tune).

\paragraph{\revtext{Pretrain$\rightarrow$Reptile}} \revtext{Reptile meta-training (300 outer steps, as above) initialised from the concat-source pre-trained weights instead of from a random initialisation. Together with Fine-tune, Fine-tune (balanced) and Reptile it completes a $2\times2$ design, \{size-weighted, basin-balanced\}~$\times$~\{pooled, episodic\}, evaluated on identical partitions and support draws.}

\paragraph{FOMAML (First-Order MAML)} For 300 outer steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Sample task $\tau$ \revtext{(a source basin, uniformly)}, support $S$, query $Q$.
    \item Inner: 5 SGD steps on $S$, lr $10^{-2}$.
    \item Compute query loss on the \emph{adapted} model.
    \item Take query-loss gradient w.r.t. adapted parameters; copy this gradient to the meta-model.
    \item Apply Adam outer-step at lr $10^{-3}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{DANN} \citep{ganin2015dann}. Feature extractor $F$: \revtext{$9 \to 64 \to 32$} (shared). Class head $C$: $32 \to 1$. Domain head $D$: $32 \to 10$ (with Gradient Reversal Layer). Trained for 100 epochs at batch 64, lr $10^{-3}$, with annealed $\lambda(p) = 2/(1+e^{-10p}) - 1$. After training, $F+C$ are loaded into the MLP backbone for $K$-shot adaptation following the same protocol as Fine-tune.

\paragraph{ProtoNet} \citep{snell2017protonet}. Prototypical networks. A shared feature encoder (same \revtext{$9 \to 64 \to 32$} architecture as the MLP backbone) is meta-trained episodically: for each source task, class prototypes are the mean support embeddings, and the prototypical loss is the cross-entropy of negative squared-Euclidean distances from query embeddings to prototypes (300 outer steps, Adam at $10^{-3}$). At test time, the $K$ target support points define prototypes and query pixels are classified by nearest prototype---no gradient adaptation is required.

\paragraph{Meta-Baseline} \citep{chen2021metabaseline}. The encoder is first pre-trained with standard supervised classification on the concatenated source pool, then frozen; at test time, $K$-shot cosine-similarity prototypes classify query pixels. This tests the claim that a strong pre-trained representation with a simple metric head can match episodic meta-learning.

\paragraph{CDAN} \citep{long2018cdan}. Conditional adversarial domain adaptation. The domain classifier is conditioned on the outer product of feature embeddings and class-probability predictions (rather than features alone as in DANN), which the authors argue yields better-aligned multimodal distributions. Training and $K$-shot adaptation follow the same protocol as DANN.

\paragraph{Spatial CNN (U-Net-style) backbone} As a methodological control on the point-wise MLP, we also train a small patch-classifier CNN on multi-channel image patches. Standard dense-segmentation benchmarks are not directly applicable to sparse point-inventory susceptibility, which has neither pixel-level masks nor bounding boxes; we therefore adopt a patch-classifier formulation that predicts per-point susceptibility from a local feature window. For each inventory point we extract a $32 \times 32$ window (covering $\sim 960 \times 960$~m at 30~m resolution) from the 25 gridded feature rasters (terrain + hydrology + texture + focal statistics) consistently available across all basins \revtext{(this experiment does not use the CFS-selected features and is therefore unaffected by the feature-selection change of Section~\ref{sec:data:cfs})}. The architecture is three Conv--ReLU blocks (32, 32, 64 channels) with two MaxPool layers, an adaptive global average pool, and a 32-unit FC head producing a binary logit ($\sim 74{,}000$ parameters). Reptile, FOMAML, Fine-tune (concat-source pre-train) and Independent are applied to this backbone under the same spatial $K$-shot CV protocol on the largest target basin (Huasco), with five fewer episodes per fold (15 vs.\ 20) to keep compute bounded. Magallanes was excluded from the source pool for this experiment because its raster stack exceeds the worker memory budget. Results (Section~\ref{sec:results:cnn}) quantify the value of spatial context for sparse-inventory susceptibility transfer.

\paragraph{Classical ML baselines} For comparison, we evaluate four classical algorithms under identical $K$-shot spatial-CV protocol: \textbf{logistic regression} (sklearn, $L_2$ regularization, $C=1.0$, 500 iterations); \textbf{random forest} (100 estimators, max depth 10); \textbf{XGBoost} (100 estimators, max depth 5, lr 0.1); \textbf{CatBoost} (100 iterations, depth 5, lr 0.1). Each algorithm is run in two modes: \emph{independent} (trained only on $K$ target samples) and \emph{concat-source} (trained on full source pool + $K$ target samples).

\paragraph{\revtext{Pre-training budgets}} \revtext{All source-trained initialisations see the source pool for a comparable number of parameter updates: 300 full-batch Adam steps for the two pooled pre-trainings, 300 outer steps (each with 5 inner steps on 20 samples) for Reptile, FOMAML and ProtoNet, and 100 epochs at batch 64 for DANN and CDAN. Meta-training a tabular MLP on 8{,}254 source samples takes seconds of CPU time per seed for every method, so no method is favoured by compute.}

\paragraph{Statistical protocol for extended baselines} ProtoNet, Meta-Baseline, and CDAN are evaluated under the same spatial-CV protocol as the primary methods with \textbf{five seeds} $\{42, 123, 7, 1729, 2718\}$, yielding 16{,}380 additional $K$-shot adaptations.

\subsection{$K$-shot evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:methods:protocol}

Throughout, $K$ denotes the number of support examples \emph{per class}: each $K$-shot episode uses $K$ labelled positives and $K$ labelled negatives ($2K$ support samples in total), so that $K=1$ corresponds to a single verified mass-movement footprint plus one non-landslide pixel. For each (target basin, $K$, method, seed) combination, we run two evaluation protocols:

\paragraph{Random cross-validation} $K$-shot episodes with support and query sampled at random from the basin's pixel pool; spatial proximity is not enforced. \revtext{The query is a balanced random draw of 10 positives and 10 negatives disjoint from the support.} The realized benchmark comprises 40 episodes per (target, $K$, method, seed) \revtext{with three seeds}; Copiap\'o is restricted to $K \in \{1, 5\}$ by its 19-event inventory, yielding 8{,}400 adaptations in total. \revtext{Random CV is reported only as the permissive foil against which the spatial protocol is compared.}

\paragraph{Spatial cross-validation} The target basin is partitioned into 5 spatial clusters via K-means on (x, y) UTM coordinates. For each fold (held-out cluster), 20 episodes sample $K$ positives + $K$ negatives from the \emph{training} clusters as support; the query is the \emph{full held-out cluster}, \revtext{which is neither balanced nor of fixed size: valid held-out clusters contain between 6 and 265 pixels and their positive share ranges from 3\% to 86\% with the local inventory density (Table~\ref{tab:fold_diagnostics})}. This guarantees support and query are spatially disjoint, addressing spatial autocorrelation per \citet{brenning2005spatial,pohjankukka2017spatialcv,valavi2019blockcv}. Fold~$\times$~seed partitions whose held-out cluster lacks both classes, or whose training clusters cannot supply $K$ positives and $K$ negatives, yield no valid episodes and are skipped. \revtext{With five seeds, the number of valid partitions is Elqui 25/25, Limar\'i 22/25, Copiap\'o 15/25 and Huasco 10/25; every exclusion is a single-class held-out cluster, and the excluded clusters are the low-relief, event-free parts of Huasco and Limar\'i (slope standard deviation 1.7 vs.\ 6.7 in Huasco; Table~\ref{tab:fold_diagnostics}, Figure~\ref{fig:fold_partitions}). The reported metrics therefore describe the event-bearing, mountainous parts of each basin, where $F_1$ is defined, rather than the basin as a whole.} We acknowledge that K-means folds do not enforce a block size larger than the empirical variogram range of model residuals; per the framework of \citet{meyer2021aoa}, this implies that reported metrics describe interpolation within the basin's training extent rather than extrapolation. \revtext{Section~\ref{sec:results:spatialindep} measures the separation between query and support pixels directly and repeats the benchmark with exclusion buffers of 2--20~km around the held-out cluster.}

Each episode:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Sample support $S$ ($K$ positives + $K$ negatives).
    \item For meta-trained methods (Reptile, FOMAML, DANN), load the corresponding meta-model; otherwise initialize from scratch.
    \item Adapt with 30 Adam steps on $S$.
    \item Evaluate $F_1$ and ROC-AUC on the query.
\end{enumerate}

\revtext{The random-number stream of every episode is seeded deterministically from (seed, fold, $K$, target), so that all methods evaluated in one script see the same support draws and every run is reproducible.}

$F_1$ is computed at a fixed decision threshold of $0.5$ on the query; because operational susceptibility mapping faces extreme class imbalance, this $F_1$ overstates the precision a practitioner would observe. We therefore also report the threshold- and prevalence-independent ROC-AUC in the main text, and a dedicated prevalence-robust analysis (precision and average precision re-weighted to realistic landslide prevalences; Section~\ref{sec:results:prevalence}).

\paragraph{\revtext{Replication structure and uncertainty}} \revtext{The replication structure is seed $\rightarrow$ spatial partition $\rightarrow$ fold $\rightarrow$ episode. Each seed draws a new K-means partition and a new meta-model, but partitions of different seeds can coincide: the adjusted Rand index between seeds is 1.00 in Huasco (identical partitions), 0.93 in Copiap\'o, 0.84 in Elqui and 0.70 in Limar\'i (Table~\ref{tab:fold_diagnostics}). Within one $(\text{seed},\text{fold})$ partition, all 20 episodes evaluate on the same held-out cluster and draw supports from the same training pool, so episodes are not independent, and the effective number of spatial replicates lies between the number of folds (5) and the number of $(\text{seed},\text{fold})$ partitions (10--25 per target), not at the nominal 200--500 runs. Every interval and test in this paper is therefore computed at the partition level: (i) all $F_1$ means in Table~\ref{tab:spatial_results} and in the spatial-CV figures carry \textbf{two-stage hierarchical-bootstrap} 95\% intervals, which resample $(\text{seed},\text{fold})$ partitions with replacement and then episodes within each resampled partition (2{,}000 resamples); the pooled intervals that treat episodes as independent are a median $3.3\times$ narrower and are reported only in the supplement; (ii) differences between methods are assessed with a \textbf{cluster-level paired test}, in which the per-partition mean-$F_1$ difference between a method and Independent, evaluated on the same held-out cluster, is one paired observation (Table~\ref{tab:cluster_paired}); (iii) as a sensitivity analysis we also average seeds within each fold and treat the fold as the unit ($n=5$ or fewer per target), the most conservative reading.}

\paragraph{\revtext{Pre-specified hypothesis and multiple comparisons}} \revtext{The primary hypothesis is a single joint statement: \emph{Reptile exceeds Independent at $K=1$ under spatial cross-validation, across the four target basins}. It is tested with a permutation test stratified by target: the sign of every partition-level paired difference is flipped at random within its target (under the null the method labels of a shared partition are exchangeable), the statistic is the mean over targets of the per-target mean difference, so that each basin has equal weight irrespective of its number of partitions, and $p$ is computed over 20{,}000 permutations. Per-target comparisons for Reptile and FOMAML are secondary (eight tests) and are Holm-corrected; Fine-tune and DANN are reported descriptively. Comparisons at $K=5$ and $K=10$ use the same paired procedure.}

The main spatial-CV $K$-shot benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:spatial_results}) nominally spans 4~targets~$\times$~4~$K$~values~$\times$~5~methods~$\times$~\revtext{5~seeds}~$\times$~5~folds~$\times$~20~episodes $= $ \revtext{40{,}000} adaptations; after excluding Copiap\'o $K=20$ and infeasible fold~$\times$~seed partitions, the realized benchmark comprises \revtext{27{,}300} $K$-shot adaptations. Adding the random-CV benchmark ($8{,}400$ adaptations), the classical-ML baselines ($29{,}520$ fits under both CV protocols and both train modes), and the extended meta-learning baselines ($16{,}380$ adaptations) yields \revtext{81{,}600} $K$-shot model fits; \revtext{the controls introduced in this revision (domain-weighting design, buffered validation, leakage quantification) add a further 134{,}100, for 215{,}700 in total}, complemented by the adaptation-curve experiment and the hyperparameter-sensitivity ablation grid.
